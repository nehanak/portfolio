Case study

Student Support Navigator

Lead designer and developer · First author research, ACM SIGCSE 2026 · Georgia Tech

React, Vite, FastAPI, Anthropic API, Qualtrics · Deployed on Vercel and Render

Live tool Source

  1. The problem

    CS students who are struggling usually know it long before they ask anyone. The gap isn't a lack of resources. It's that naming the problem out loud, to a professor or a TA, feels like an admission. Existing support systems all start at the point where a student has already decided to speak up.

  2. Research

    I ran a mixed methods study on a research platform I built in Qualtrics: 200+ survey responses across 45 institutions, capturing where students get stuck and who, if anyone, they tell. The dominant pattern was students misreading a normal, widely shared difficulty as a personal, disqualifying one.

  3. Design decisions

    I designed the interaction around reflection rather than referral. A student describes what's going on in their own words; the tool classifies it against the research taxonomy and reflects back what it heard, including how common that experience was in the dataset. No account, no advisor loop, no stored identity, making this the lowest stakes possible entry point. The classifier's output shapes the tone of the response, not just its content.

  4. Outcome

    The classifier reached 85% accuracy (Cohen's κ = 0.774) after several rounds of prompt iteration against hand labeled responses. The tool is live and the underlying research was accepted to ACM SIGCSE 2026 as a first author paper on CS curriculum reform.

Case study

Bridge Forward

Lead designer and developer · Built with Common Good and Georgia Works

React, Vite (mobile first PWA), FastAPI, SQLite, Claude tool calling

In progress, prototyping

  1. The problem

    Through Common Good I mentor formerly incarcerated adults at Georgia Works. Reentry is a set of long, interlocking processes: ID documents, GED, job applications, financial aid. Each has its own deadlines and failure points. Mentors carry all of that in their heads for a dozen people at once, and progress quietly stalls between check ins.

  2. Research

    I interviewed reentry stakeholders and mentors to map where cases actually break down. Two constraints came out of it that shaped everything: most participants have a phone and nothing else, and typing a long message is a much bigger ask than tapping a button.

  3. Design decisions

    Two views of one case: a participant check in built primarily from taps with short text only where it's genuinely needed, and a mentor dashboard that surfaces flags rather than waiting to be read. Goals use fixed preset categories, so anything that doesn't fit routes to a mentor instead of being silently bucketed as "other." The agent can improvise wording but not invent new action types, and anything legal or mental health related requires human review before it reaches a participant.

  4. Where it stands

    Backend and frontend are scaffolded and I'm prototyping the check in flow against four validated goal categories: college, financial aid, jobs, and GED prep. The open design question is how much the agent should say before a mentor has seen it.

Case study

Atlanta Food Finder

Five person team · Agile/Scrum, tracked in Jira

Django, JavaScript, Google Maps API

Source

  1. The problem

    Restaurant search returns a list when what people actually want is a decision. Filters exist across most food apps, but they're stacked in ways that make the last step, picking one place and getting there, take the most effort.

  2. Design decisions

    We built search and map as one view rather than two tabs, so narrowing by cuisine, rating, price, or distance changes what's on the map immediately. Each listing carries only what a decision needs: hours, cuisine, rating, address. Reviews and directions sit one step away instead of inline.

  3. Working as a team

    Five contributors on a two week sprint cadence, with scope managed in Jira. Most of my design effort went into arguing scope down: the feature list we started with wouldn't have shipped, and the version that did is narrower and clearer for it.

  4. Outcome

    Shipped as a working application with search, filtering, map view, reviews, and directions. 40 commits across the team.
